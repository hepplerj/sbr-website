## Scope and selection

The corpus of materials associated with land use management in the American West is vast. *Governing Ground* uses [Documents](/documents) as an annotated archive of primary sources relevant to the research and argument being developed in the project. The document archive is intentional and selective, relying largely on public laws, court filings, hearing transcripts, executive orders, and when possible secondary sources that I have permission to share. As new details come to light about relevant documents, people, or events, I refine the interpretive apparatus. *Governing Ground*'s archive is, of necessity, selective rather than comprehensive.

## Sourcing and permissions

The bulk of items in the archive as of June 5, 2026, include public domain government works that are openly available for use (federal statutes, court opinions, congressional hearings, executive orders). Materials I've transcribed from private collections are only available if I have explicit permission to share the materials from the rights holder.

## Transcription practices

*Governing Ground* transcribes and processes material such as handwritten correspondence, government documents, court records, public laws, and other materials either from scanned images of original material or from publicly available transcriptions if they are available. 

Drawing on Association for Documentary Editing guidelines, *Governing Ground* seeks to render the documents as accurately as possible. The transcriptions retain all original spelling, syntax, and grammar. 

## Annotation conventions

Annotations cover a range of information, including notes on provenance, textual notes, and scholarly interpretation. Contextual notes are included to explain references to people, places, and events not readily identified through standard references. 

Annotations come in two modes:

- **Glosses** (highlighted spans + marginal sidenotes)---short editorial commentary providing context, cross-references, definitions, or interpretive flagging at the level of a phrase or sentence. Visible in the document body as a rust-underlined span; full text appears in the right-rail sidenote column on wide screens, or as an inline expander on narrow screens.

- **Footnotes** (numbered references at the bottom of the document)---longer analytical commentary, source criticism, comparison with related scholarship, or material that's too lengthy for marginalia.
