## Cattle and livestock

### Animal unit month (AUM)
The forage one mature cow with calf (or one horse, or five sheep or goats) needs for one month.

### Beef cow
A mature female bovine kept for breeding in a beef-cattle (rather than dairy) operation.

### Brand
A permanent ownership mark, traditionally hot-iron-burned into the hide. 

### Bull
An intact (unaltered) adult male bovine, kept for breeding.

### Calf
A bovine of either sex under approximately one year old.

### Cow
A mature female bovine that has produced at least one calf. (A female bovine that has not yet calved is a *heifer*.)

### Cow-calf operation
A breeding herd of cows on grass, calving each spring, and selling weaned calves in the fall.

### Cwt (hundredweight)
A hundred pounds. The conventional unit for quoting cattle prices.

### Feedlot
A concentrated operation that finishes cattle on grain to market weight.

### Finishing
The grain-feeding phase that takes a yearling steer or heifer from grass weight (~700–800 lbs) to slaughter weight (1,200–1,400 lbs).

### Head
A single animal.

### Heifer
A young female bovine that has not yet produced a calf.

### Herd
The collective set of cattle a rancher manages.

### Stocking rate
The number of animal units a piece of range can support without degradation, usually expressed in AUMs per acre.

### Steer
A male bovine castrated young and raised for beef.

## Range and grazing

### Allotment
A specific named parcel of federal range on which a rancher or a grazing association holds a permit to graze a specific class and number of livestock for a specific season.

### Deferred grazing
A range-management practice of resting a pasture from grazing for part or all of a season to allow vegetation recovery.

### Grazing district
The administrative unit one level above the allotment. The Taylor Grazing Act of 1934 organized the public range into grazing districts (originally Department of Grazing, then Grazing Service, now BLM); each district contains many allotments and is staffed by its own BLM office.

### Grazing fee
The per-AUM price the federal government charges permittees to graze on BLM and USFS land. Set by a 1978 statutory formula that ties the fee to private-lease rates and cattle prices.

### In-holding
A parcel of private land surrounded by federal land, usually a survival of the railroad-land-grant checkerboard, the Homestead Act, or earlier mining patents.

### Multiple use
The statutory mandate that federal rangelands and forests be managed for a balance of grazing, timber, mineral leasing, recreation, watershed protection, and wildlife habitat.

### Permit
The formal authorization that lets a rancher graze a specified number of animals on a specified allotment for a specified season.

### Public domain
Historically, the federal land that had passed to the United States from foreign sovereigns (treaty, purchase, or cession) and not yet been transferred to private, state, or tribal ownership.

### Rest rotation
A grazing-management system that takes part of an allotment out of use each year on rotation, so each pasture gets a full year (or longer) of rest periodically.

### Sustained yield
Companion to *multiple use*: the principle that federal lands should be managed so the resources they produce (forage, timber, etc.) can continue indefinitely.

## Federal lands and land measurement

### Acre
43,560 square feet, or 1/640 of a square mile. 

### Disposal
Transfer of federal land out of federal ownership.

### Land grant
A grant of federal public-domain land to states, railroads, or to individuals.

### National Monument
A federal land designation under the Antiquities Act of 1906, proclaimed by the president without Congressional consent, intended to protect objects of historic or scientific interest.

### National forest
Land administered by the US Forest Service. The system began with the Forest Reserve Act of 1891 and now totals ~193 million acres across 154 forests and 20 grasslands.

### National grassland
Forest Service-administered grassland created from former Bankhead-Jones Farm Tenant Act (1937) submarginal-farmland acquisitions, formally designated as grasslands by Secretary Benson in 1960.

### National park
Land set aside by Congress and administered by the National Park Service under the 1916 Organic Act.

### Quarter section
160 acres, or one-fourth of a section. The standard Homestead Act parcel. 

### Section
One square mile (640 acres). The basic survey unit of the federal rectangular cadastral system established by the 1785 Land Ordinance. Each township contains 36 numbered sections.

### Township
A six-mile-square block (36 square miles) in the federal rectangular survey, the level above sections. Townships are numbered by their distance north or south of a baseline and east or west of a meridian.

### Wilderness area
A federal-land designation under the 1964 Wilderness Act: lands kept "untrammeled by man," with no roads, motorized vehicles, mechanized equipment, timber harvest, or new mining claims. Designated by Congress.

### Withdrawal
Removal of a parcel of federal land from one set of public land laws by executive or congressional action.
