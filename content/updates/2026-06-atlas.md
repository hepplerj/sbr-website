The site has a new section: the **[Atlas](/atlas/)**. Where a sightline poses an argument with a single visualization, an atlas entry hands you the apparatus to ask your own questions — hover, click through, sort, drill down. The two are complementary, used differently.

## The first entry

**[Regional Congressional Cosponsorships, 96th–119th Congress](/atlas/regional-trends/)** charts twenty-four Congresses of cosponsorship activity (1979–2026) across six CRS policy areas, broken out by three regional delegations — Great Plains, American West, and the Midwest as a federal-land-free contrast case — and by chamber.

The view is built to be interrogated:

- **Hover** any panel for a guide-line at the nearest Congress, with the year span and each region's value.
- **Click** to pin a Congress; a detail panel opens below with per-region values, bill counts, and party breakdowns.
- From there, drill into a **member list** with name search and state-filter chips — useful for asking which members drove a particular spike.
- **Metric**, **chamber**, and **region-legend** controls reframe the same data without leaving the page.

The unit of analysis is the delegation as a political-economic actor, which is why this three-region scheme differs from the eight-region one the sightlines filter uses — a different question wants a different boundary.

## Presentation

The Atlas list and entry pages share the sightlines design system — a section-intro header, card grid with optional thumbnails, and matching typography — so the two sections read as siblings.

The visualization is complete; the accompanying prose is still in draft, and this changelog will note when it's finished.
