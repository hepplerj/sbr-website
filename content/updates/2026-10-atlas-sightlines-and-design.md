The changelog has been quiet since June, but the site has not. This entry catches up on what changed between then and now.

## The Atlas

The [Atlas](/atlas/) began with a single instrument. It now has five:

- **[The Federal Footprint, State by State](/atlas/federal-footprint/)** ranks seventeen states by how much of their land carries a federal interest. Sort by fee-title ownership and you get the familiar story, with Nevada, Utah, and Idaho on top. Sort by *total* federal interest, counting tribal trust land and federal easements on private land, and the ranking changes: Arizona climbs to third, and South Dakota jumps from about 5% to nearly half.
- **[One Bill, Seven Statehouses](/atlas/legislation-textreuse/)** compares the state sagebrush public-lands bills of 1979–1982 as text. Each cell of the matrix gives the share of five-word phrases two bills have in common; click one to read the passages they share.
- **[The Producer's Vocabulary, 1919–1961](/atlas/producer-vocabulary/)** charts term frequencies across the full run of the *American Cattle Producer*, grouped into seven concepts, from aridity and range condition to anti-federal rhetoric.
- **[Reading the Producer: A Concordance](/atlas/producer-concordance/)** is the companion: pick a term and read every occurrence in context, filtered by year, with each line linked to the page scan at the Internet Archive.

Each Atlas entry now has a thumbnail on the [list page](/atlas/), as sightlines do.

## Sightlines

- **[A Drying West](/sightlines/precipitation-history/)** now charts precipitation for the eleven western public-land states instead of the contiguous United States. The national series ran *above* its 20th-century average after 2000, because the Northeast and Midwest got wetter, which hid the drought in the West. The western series averages about 0.8 inches below normal since 2000, and 2020 is the driest year in its record. A new band marks the megadrought, and the event table was recomputed for the same states.
- **[Federal Easements on the Northern Plains](/sightlines/easements-over-time/)** is new: Fish & Wildlife Service easement tracts on private land across the Dakotas, Montana, Wyoming, and Colorado, by the year the United States acquired them.
- **[Farm Foreclosures, 1913–1981](/sightlines/farm-foreclosures/)** is new: the federal government's own count of farms lost to foreclosure.
- **[US Federal Lands by Agency](/sightlines/us-federal-lands/)** now adds tribal trust land, federal easements, and the Great Plains outline to the fee-title map, with a readout of every federal interest under the cursor.
- **[Federal Lands in the American West](/sightlines/federal-lands/)** is published.
- The *Follow the Money* chart and map are retired now that the project is live again at its own home, [whopaysthewest.com](https://whopaysthewest.com).

## Design

- The [home page](/) has a project introduction, a start-here list, recent work, and plates for each section.
- Page headers carry an 1878 General Land Office survey map in the background.
- Essays and changelog entries now share one layout, with a header that gives the author, date, and reading time on a single line. Essays are bylined to Jason Heppler. Changelog entries, which are drafted with generative AI, are bylined to Claude, alongside the disclosure note each one already carried.
- The [statement on generative AI](/ai/) now lists which Claude models the project has used, and when.
