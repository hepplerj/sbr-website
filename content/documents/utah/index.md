**Enrolled Copy | S. B. No. 5**

**1980 Budget Session**

_By Ivan M. Matheson, E. Verl Asay, William N. Jones, Sherman A. Wayment, Jack M. Bangerter, Eldon A. Money, Miles 'Cap' Ferry_

**AN ACT relating to state land; providing for state control of public land within the boundaries of Utah; providing authority in the division of state lands to administer public land; providing for creation of the state public land committee, its composition and duties; providing a penalty; and providing an effective date.**

**BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF UTAH:**

### Section 1. Public Policy

The public land of the State of Utah is one of its greatest assets and must be administered in such a way as to conserve and preserve the state's natural resources, wildlife habitat, wilderness areas, and historical sites and artifacts while allowing the development of compatible uses of such land for recreation purposes, mining and timber production, agricultural and ranching pursuits, and for the development, production, and transmission of energy and other public utility services under principles of multiple use which provide the greatest benefit to the citizens of this state.

### Section 2. Definitions

As used in this chapter:

(1) "Board" means the board of state lands created by section 65-1-1.

(2) "Division" means the division of state lands/forestry created by section 65-1-2.1.

(3) "Public land" means all land located within the boundaries of this state and all minerals on or below the surface of such land, except:

(a) Land which is titled and held in the name of a private individual or entity;

(b) Land which is titled and held in the name of the state, its political subdivisions, units of local government, or any institution within the state system of higher education;

(c) Land located within a national park, national monument, national forest or wildlife refuge, or which is acquired by Congress with the consent of the state legislature;

(d) Land which is used by the United States Department of Defense or Department of Energy; and

(e) Land reserved and held in trust as Indian reservations or for Indian purposes.

(4) "State land maintenance account" means the restricted revenue account created within the uniform school fund by section 65-1-13.

### Section 3. Trust Responsibilities

The division shall hold all public land in trust for the benefit of the people of the state and is vested with authority, subject to the conditions imposed upon the exercise of such authority by this act, to administer and manage such land consistent with the policy declared in section 1.

### Section 4. Title Vesting and Federal Laws

(1) Subject to rights existing on the effective date of this act, fee title to all public land not previously appropriated is vested in the state from and after the effective date of this act.

(2) Until equivalent measures are enacted, the rights and privileges of the people of the State of Utah granted under the provisions of existing federal law (including the National Forest Reserve Transfer Act, General Mining Laws, Homestead Act, Taylor Grazing Act, Desert Land Act, Carey Act, and Public Rangelands Improvements Act) and all rights-of-way and easements for public utilities must be preserved under administration by the state.

(3) Public land in the state which has been administered by the United States under international treaties or interstate compacts shall be administered by the division in conformance with those treaties or compacts.

### Section 5. Fees, Rentals, and Revenue

(1) No sale after July 1, 1980, of public land by the division is authorized unless specifically authorized by an act of the legislature.

(2) The board shall set the fees, rentals, royalties, interest, and penalties charged for the use of public land in accordance with the standards and rates established for the school trust lands in the state.

(3) All revenue derived from fees, rents, royalties, interest, forfeitures, or other money paid to the state shall be deposited with the state treasurer for credit to the state general fund. The state treasurer shall deposit 20% of the revenue so received to the state land maintenance account to be used by the board in the administration of non-trust public land.

### Section 6. Written Authorization and Penalties

(1) Any person who intends to perform or who actually performs any act with respect to the use, management, or disposal of public land under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom or usage of the United States, or otherwise, shall obtain written authorization from the division approving or confirming such act.

(2) Any person who fails to obtain written authorization from the division as required by subsection (1) of this section, may be enjoined by the division in a court of competent jurisdiction.

(3) Any person who receives any money or other consideration from the purported sale or other disposition of public land made contrary to the provisions of section 5 or 6 is liable in damages to the state.

### Section 7. State Public Land Committee

(1) There is hereby created a state public land committee composed of the members of the board, the executive director of the department of natural resources, the director of the division, and two members appointed by the governor from each district specified in section 65-1-1.1 who are elected and serving officials of local governments located within each such district.

(3) The state public land committee shall conduct a study of the public land in the state to determine which land should be made available to local governments for public works and recreation projects, for public sale to private individuals or entities, and for retention as habitats for wildlife, state parks, recreation sites, and other public uses.

(4) The state public land committee shall prepare and submit a written report of its recommendations for the disposition of the public land in the state to the governor and the legislature not later than three years after the final adjudication in subsection (6) below.

(5) No public land proposed to be retained by the state for wildlife, parks, recreation, or other public uses shall be transferred to the administering state agency without the prior approval of the legislature.

(6) All duties made incumbent upon the division of state lands, the board of state lands, and the state public land committee by this act shall become effective only after there is a final adjudication that the act is constitutional.

### Section 8. Exclusive Legal Authority

The attorney general is vested with exclusive authority to commence any action to protect the interest of the state in its public land against claims of the federal government to such land or to defend any such action brought by the federal government.

### Section 9. Class B Misdemeanor

Any person who attempts to exercise jurisdiction over the public land contrary to the laws of this state is guilty of a class B misdemeanor.

### Section 10. Effective Date

This act shall take effect July 1, 1980.
