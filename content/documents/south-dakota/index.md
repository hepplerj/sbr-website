**Senate Bill No. 131 — Fifty-Sixth Session, Legislative Assembly, 1981**

_Introduced by Senators Allen, Brown, Manke, Lyndell Petersen and Samuelson and Representatives Marsden, Freeman, Jorgensen, Kocer, Lyon, Pederson and Van Gerpen_

**FOR AN ACT ENTITLED, An Act to provide for the orderly transfer of certain federally-held property to the state of South Dakota.**

**BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF SOUTH DAKOTA:**

### Section 1. Definitions

Terms, as used in this Act, unless the context otherwise clearly requires, mean:

1. "Board," the board of review established by section 2 of this Act;

2. "Department," the department of school and public lands;

3. "Public lands," all land within the exterior boundaries of the state of South Dakota except lands:

    (a) To which title is held by any private person or entity;

    (b) To which title is held by the state of South Dakota or any of its units of local government;

    (c) Which are located within congressionally authorized national parks, monuments, national forests or wildlife refuges, or which are lands acquired by purchase consented to by the Legislature;

    (d) Which are declared reserved by the United States Department of Defense, Department of Energy, Bureau of Reclamation, Army Corp of Engineers or United States Postal Service;

    (e) Which are held in trust for Indian purposes or are Indian reservations.

### Section 2. Board of Review

There is hereby created a board of review composed of the following:

1. The secretary of the department of water and natural resources;

2. The secretary of the department of agriculture;

3. The secretary of the department of game, fish and parks;

4. The state engineer;

5. The chairman of the state conservation commission;

6. The chairman of the state senate standing committee on state affairs;

7. The chairman of the state house of representatives standing committee on state affairs; and

8. The commissioner of school and public lands is an ex officio member of the board.

### Section 3. Chairman and Quorum

The chairman of the senate standing committee on state affairs shall serve as chairman of the board. The board shall meet at such times and places as are specified by a call of the chairman. Five members of the board constitute a quorum and an affirmative vote of the members present is sufficient for any action of the board.

### Section 4. Board Review of Rules and Decisions

The board shall review and approve or disapprove all rules proposed by the commissioner of school and public lands pursuant to section 6 of this Act. The board of review may also review any decision of the commissioner of school and public lands made under the provisions of this Act if an appeal is taken pursuant to section 20 of this Act, and affirm, modify or reverse the decision.

### Section 5. Trust and Management

The department shall hold the public lands of the state in trust for the benefit of the people of the state and shall manage them in an orderly and beneficial manner consistent with the public policy declared in section 12 of this Act.

### Section 6. Rulemaking

The commissioner of school and public lands may with the approval of the board adopt, pursuant to chapter 1-26, rules necessary to manage the public lands in an orderly and beneficial manner and to carry out the provisions of this Act and the public trust created by this Act.

### Section 7. Personnel

The commissioner of school and public lands may contract for or employ professional and clerical personnel as are needed to carry out his functions. Any contract for professional services must be approved by the state board of finance and any money necessary to compensate those persons must be approved for expenditure by the Legislature pursuant to chapters 4-8 or 4-8A.

### Section 8. Property of State

Subject to existing rights, all public lands in South Dakota and all minerals not previously appropriated are the property of the state of South Dakota and subject to its jurisdiction and control.

### Section 9. Preservation of Existing Rights

Until equivalent measures are enacted by the state of South Dakota, the rights and privileges of the people of the state of South Dakota under the National Forest Reserve Transfer Act (16 U.S.C. §§ 471 et seq.), the General Mining Laws (30 U.S.C. § 21 et seq.), the Homestead Act (43 U.S.C. §§ 161 et seq.), the Taylor Grazing Act (43 U.S.C. §§ 315 et seq.), the Desert Land Act (43 U.S.C. §§ 321 et seq.), the Carey Act (43 U.S.C. §§ 641 et seq.), the Public Rangelands Improvement Act (43 U.S.C. §§ 1901 et seq.), and any other federal law and all rights-of-way and easements for public utilities shall be preserved under administration by the state.

### Section 10. Treaties and Compacts

Public lands in South Dakota which have been administered by the United States under international treaties or interstate compacts must continue to be administered by the state in conformance with those treaties or compacts.

### Section 11. Payments in Lieu of Taxes

The commissioner shall continue to pay in lieu of tax payments to units of local government in the same manner, using the same method, as the federal government agencies use prior to the transfer of public lands.

### Section 12. Administration Policy

The public lands of South Dakota must be administered in such a manner as to conserve and preserve natural resources, wildlife habitat, wilderness areas, historical sites and artifacts, and to permit the development of compatible public uses for agriculture, ranching, recreation, mining and timber production and the development, production and transmission of energy and other public utility services under principles of multiple use which provide the greatest benefit to the people of South Dakota.

### Section 13. Disposal Restricted

Except as provided in section 14 of this Act, no sale, conveyance or other disposal of the public lands may be permitted or authorized by the commissioner of school and public lands, unless specifically authorized by the act of the Legislature enacted after July 1, 1981.

### Section 14. Conveyance to Extent of Federal Practice

To the extent that the public lands may be conveyed, leased, permitted or licensed by the federal government or any of its agencies, the commissioner of school and public lands is hereby authorized to convey, lease, license or permit the use of public lands to the same extent or in the same manner as those lands are conveyed, leased, licensed or permitted to be used by the federal government or any of its agencies.

### Section 15. Proceeds

Subject to the provisions of section 11 of this Act, all proceeds of fees, rents, royalties or other money paid to the state under section 14 of this Act shall be deposited with the state treasurer for credit to the state general fund.

### Section 16. Unauthorized Disposal Void

Except as it is authorized pursuant to section 9 of this Act or except as it may be authorized by the commissioner of school and public lands pursuant to any authority conferred upon him by law, any sale, lease, exchange, encumbrance or other disposal of any parcel of or any interest in the public lands is void.

### Section 17. Written Authorization Required

Any person who intends to perform or who actually carries out any act with respect to the use, management or disposal of any of the public lands under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom or usage of the United States or otherwise, shall obtain written authorization from the commissioner of school and public lands approving or confirming any such act, which authorization shall be given only to the extent it is authorized under the laws of this state.

### Section 18. Injunction

Any person who does not obtain written authorization from the commissioner of school and public lands as required by section 17 may be enjoined by the commissioner of school and public lands from attempting to perform or continuing to carry out any act respecting the use, management or disposal of any of the public lands in any court of competent jurisdiction of this state within whose jurisdiction any of the affected public lands are located or the person resides.

### Section 19. Recovery of Proceeds

Any person who receives any money or other consideration for any purported sale or other disposition of any public land which was made contrary to the provisions of section 9 or 16 of this Act is liable to the state for that money or for the value of any other consideration. The money may be recovered in an action brought by the commissioner of school and public lands in a court of competent jurisdiction of this state within whose jurisdiction any of the affected public lands are located or the person resides.

### Section 20. Appeals

Any person who is aggrieved by a decision of the commissioner of school and public lands made pursuant to this Act may appeal by letter to the board within thirty days after the date of the decision from which the appeal is taken. The letter must set out:

1. The decision from which the appeal is taken;

2. Legal grounds for the contention of the appellant that the decision exceeds the authority of the commissioner of school and public lands; and

3. Facts to support the contention, with sufficient particularity to permit the commissioner of school and public lands to prepare for a hearing.

### Section 21. Board Action on Appeal

Upon receiving the letter, the board may:

1. Dismiss the appeal if it appears from the letter to lack any merit; or

2. Set a date for a hearing of the appeal which must be not less than fifteen days nor more than forty-five days after the date on which the board receives the letter. The board shall notify the commissioner of school and public lands and the appellant of the date, time and place of the hearing. Any hearing held by the board must be informal.

### Section 22. Hearing Procedure

The commissioner of school and public lands or his representative shall present at the hearing the facts considered in reaching his decision. The appellant or his representative may present matters in support of his contention that the commissioner's decision exceeds his authority. If the appellant does not appear in person or by representative, the board may consider the matters set forth in his letter of appeal and may dismiss the appeal or take any other action which it finds to be reasonable and proper. The board shall issue its order as soon as practicable after conducting the hearing. The order of the board is a final decision in a contested case.

### Section 23. Role of Attorney General

The attorney general may initiate or defend any action commenced in any court to carry out or enforce the provisions of this Act or seek any appropriate judicial relief to protect the interests of the state or the people of the state in the public lands. The right to enforce the provisions of this Act vests exclusively in the attorney general.

### Section 24. Study and Inventory

The department of water and natural resources shall conduct an inventory and a study of the public lands of South Dakota to determine, in conjunction with the respective boards of county commissioners, the methods of management that will best satisfy the requirements of section 12 of this Act and establish a basis for determining the best multiple uses of the land.

The department of water and natural resources shall submit a report of its findings and recommendations to the session of the Legislature following the effective date of this Act.

### Section 25. Contingent Effective Date

Section 1 to 24, inclusive, of this Act shall become effective upon the passage of federal legislation granting control of public lands to the state of South Dakota.

### Section 26. Federal Acquisition Prohibited

The federal government, or any agency of the federal government, may not acquire any interest in land within the exterior boundaries of the state of South Dakota without the consent of the Legislature, except such lands as may be acquired pursuant to Article I, section 8, of the United States Constitution.

_Transcription note: A conference committee amended the bill to strike Sections 1–25 and the original title, leaving only the Section 26 prohibition on federal land acquisition ("prohibit the federal acquisition of land within South Dakota except for constitutional purposes"). The engrossed transcript above preserves the full original text as introduced. Handwritten annotations on the tracking form indicate the measure was ultimately vetoed._
