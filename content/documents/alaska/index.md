**State of Alaska Official Election Pamphlet**

**BALLOT MEASURE NO. 5**

**Initiative No. 80-01**

**CLAIMING STATE OWNERSHIP OF FEDERAL LAND**

### Summary

_(As it will appear on the November 2, 1982 General Election Ballot)_

This initiative, in effect, claims state ownership of all federal land in Alaska except Mt. McKinley National Park, national monuments established before 1977, native corporation selections, the Annette Islands Reserve, and land controlled by the Department of Defense or the Alaska Power Administration. If approved, the initiative would provide Alaska with additional grounds to join other states which have similar laws constitutionally challenging federal ownership of land within their boundaries. The proposal would have the State hold this land in trust for the people of Alaska until terms and conditions for its disposal are prescribed by law.

**Ballot Form:**

- A vote "FOR" adopts the initiative.
    
- A vote "AGAINST" rejects the initiative.
    

> **Result:** Passed by voters 11-2-82 (75% to 25%)

### Legislative Affairs Agency Summary

_(As required by law)_

The initiative claims ownership for the State of all Federal land in the State—except for Federal land controlled by the Department of Defense or the Alaska Power Administration on the effective date of the initiative, except for the Annette Islands Reserve, except for Federal land selected by a regional or village corporation under the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act, and except for land located within the Mt. McKinley National Park or within national monuments established before January 1, 1977. The initiative would require the State to hold the land in trust for the people of the State. The initiative would grant the land to the Alaska Department of Natural Resources for management. The Department of Natural Resources would not be authorized to sell, lease, exchange, or encumber the land until specifically authorized by law under terms and conditions which have been established by law. The initiative would also establish enforcement and penalty provisions.

### Full Text of Initiative

**BE IT ENACTED BY THE PEOPLE OF THE STATE OF ALASKA:**

*Section 1. AS 38.05 is amended by adding new sections to read:

#### Article 13. State Control of Certain Land

**Sec. 38.05.500. ELECTORATE DETERMINATIONS.** The people of the State of Alaska determine that:

1. the intent of the framers of the Constitution of the United States was to guarantee to each of the states sovereignty over all matters within its boundaries except for those powers specifically granted to the United States as agent of the states;
    
2. the attempted imposition upon the State of Alaska by the Congress of the United States of a requirement in the Statehood Act that the State of Alaska and its people "disclaim all right and title to any lands or other property not granted or confirmed to the state or its political subdivisions by or under the authority of this Act, the right or title to which is held by the United States or is subject to disposition by the United States," as a condition precedent to acceptance of Alaska into the Union, was an act beyond the power of the Congress of the United States and is thus void;
    
3. The purported right of ownership and control of the public land in the State of Alaska by the United States is without foundation and violates the clear intent of the Constitution of the United States; and
    
4. the exercise of that dominion and control of the public land in the State of Alaska by the United States works a severe, continuous and debilitating hardship upon the people of the State of Alaska.
    

**Sec. 38.05.510. MANAGEMENT.**

(a) The Department of Natural Resources shall manage the land which is the subject of AS 38.05.500-38.05.590. The department will use the provisions of this chapter for the management of the land except to the extent that the provisions of AS 38.05.500-38.05.590 provide to the contrary.

(b) The department shall manage the land in an orderly and beneficial manner.

(c) The department may adopt regulations for the management of the land. No regulation which imposes a limitation upon the people of the state is effective until approved by the legislature.

(d) The department may sell, lease, exchange, or encumber the land when specifically authorized to do so by law and under the terms and conditions established by the law.

**Sec. 38.05.520. PROPERTY OF THE PEOPLE.**

(a) Subject to valid existing rights of applicants for land, upon the effective date of this act, all land in the state and all minerals not previously appropriated are the exclusive property of the people of the state and the state holds title to the land and minerals in trust for the people of the state.

**Sec. 38.05.540. TREATIES AND COMPACTS.** Land in the state which has been administered by the United States under international treaties and interstate compacts will continue to be administered by the state in conformity with those treaties and compacts.

**Sec. 38.05.560. PROCEEDS TO THE GENERAL FUND.** The proceeds of sales, fees, rents, royalties or other receipts from the land paid to the state under the provisions of AS 38.05.500-38.05.590 shall be deposited in the general fund, and the legislature may provide for payments in lieu of taxes to local governments.

**Sec. 38.05.570. EXCLUSIVE ENFORCEMENT.**

(a) The state has exclusive jurisdiction to enforce the provisions of AS 38.05.500-38.05.590.

(b) An individual may institute a civil action to recover damages for injury or loss sustained as the result of a violation of the provisions of AS 38.05.500-38.05.590 or for the failure of the state to enforce its trust responsibilities to the people of the state.

### Statement in Favor of Ballot Measure No. 5

The "Tundra Rebellion" initiative follows the lead of five other western states which have passed legislation challenging federal ownership of land within their boundaries, and attempts to transfer most of that land to state government control. In other states this effort is called the "Sagebrush Rebellion".

About 93.5% of all federal land is found in twelve western states. Alaska, with the most at stake in this issue, will still have almost 70% of its land held by the federal government even after all transfers are made. Our leaders have failed to act. It's time for the people of Alaska to speak out loudly and clearly on this issue by passing the "Tundra Rebellion" initiative.

Although opponents of the initiative may try to describe the "Tundra Rebellion" issue as one of development vs. nondevelopment, that is not the case. The major issue involved is the question of self-determination for the people of Alaska. Because the federal government owns the vast majority of land in Alaska, there is hardly anything that can happen in Alaska without getting the permission of the federal government and subjecting ourselves to its demands. Our Congressional delegation may very well oppose the initiative because it would decrease federal control which they wield over Alaska. Alaskan politicians who favor a "no-growth" policy for Alaska may oppose it because the federal government is their convenient excuse for nondevelopment.

In order to evaluate the "Tundra Rebellion" initiative ask yourself the following questions:

1. Are we Alaskans more likely to have the freedom to acquire good land from a faraway federal government or from the State of Alaska over which we have some control?
    
2. Who should have more to say about what happens to Alaska's land—Alaskans, or unelected federal bureaucrats in Washington, D.C.?
    

Opponents of the initiative may try to say that Alaska is not capable of managing this land or that it will cost too much to do so. They may even say that passage would lead to ruin of Alaska's land. What they are really saying is that we Alaskans can't trust ourselves and that we can't responsibly assume more control over our own lives. To the contrary, Alaskans should have the freedom to determine their own destiny. Beware of those who ask you to fear your own freedom, for they are to be feared.

The "Tundra Rebellion" is based on constitutional principles and can help bring about self-determination for Alaskans in two ways. Firstly, its passage will give Alaska better standing to join other states who are pursuing a remedy through the courts. Secondly, it will add pressure on Congress to act favorably on legislation dealing with federal land transfers. The Reagan administration has expressed support for land transfers to states. Passage of the "Tundra Rebellion" will encourage further action. Most importantly, it will be an opportunity for Alaskans to say loud and clear that we want the self-determination to decide our own future.

_— Dick Randolph, Representative, Alaska State Legislature_

### Statement Against Ballot Measure No. 5

Passage of this initiative would be a meaningless gesture that would embarrass our state for years to come. By adoption of this initiative, the State would be trying to breach a contract we entered into with the United States in 1958 which provided statehood for Alaska. In Section 4 of that contract we agreed to a 104 million-acre land grant as the state's full entitlement to federal lands. This contract was ratified by Sections 12 and 13 of Article XII of the Alaska Constitution. Because these issues have been waived by the Statehood Act and the Alaska Constitution, any attempt to implement the provisions of this initiative, if adopted, would be illegal and unconstitutional.

It is important that we realize what effect approval of this initiative would have on the rest of the country's perception of Alaska. The recent repeal of state income tax and enactment of the Permanent Fund dividend program have drawn national attention to our current oil wealth. If this initiative passes, we will invite the image of being greedy for land, in addition to money. It will be pointed out that our existing 104 million-acre land entitlement is already the most generous grant ever given a state. A vote "FOR" this initiative will just add fuel to the effort in the US Congress to reduce Alaska's share of the oil revenues.

Our federal lands are an important part of our national heritage, and must remain open and accessible to the public. These lands provide timber, minerals, coal, oil, gas, fish, wildlife and watershed protection. Local governments receive 25 percent of all national forest revenues, while the State received 50 percent of oil and gas receipts from the National Petroleum Reserve and 90 percent of oil and gas receipts on all other federal lands in Alaska. Our state enjoys an enviable relationship with the public lands within our boundaries.

The sponsors of this initiative need to face up to the terms of the Statehood Act and current oil revenue politics. In the only state to put this issue to a public vote, our neighbor, Washington, recently defeated a similar initiative. Let's not embarrass ourselves by approving this misdirected initiative.

PLEASE VOTE "AGAINST" THIS BALLOT MEASURE.

_— David Finkelstein, Alaskans for Responsible Government_
